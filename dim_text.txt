# Introduction. Philosophy of the Dimensions.
This is a board game based on the open rules of role-play ChaosD6.
There are four Stats (Pillars), which define your identity: Vitality, World, Ethereality and Will. You may read about the four pillars of strength, used as a metamodeling to reach the defined Stats (Physical => Vitality, Spiritual => World, Emotional => Ethereality and Mental => Will).
    Physical strength: The ability to apply force or resistance during a given task.
    Emotional strength: The ability to embrace a wide array of feelings and emotions and to transition from one response to another with full awareness and confidence. Referred as Charisma, Emotional Intelligence.
    Mental strength: To be able to focus over an extended period. To formulate your own conclusions to questions based upon the ability and commitment to continued education and learning of the self.
    Spiritual strength: Is the commitment to your personal values, beliefs and World. The wisdom to understand yours and others beliefs is also the World.
Ordinary objects have zero vitality and will. Plants usually have zero willpower. Astral and undead creatures have zero vitality. Magical creatures and objects are ethereal, and those who can influence reality have a World.
We will describe our characters as a set of these four Stats.
Every living being can have all four Stats and have conscious or unconscious abilities based on their nature. Use of all the features can be increased or suppressed. A mode of use of each of these conditions is the presence of the so-called "elements".
Vital energy directly manifests the Vitality of each of us. How strong physically and how tough we are, how sharpened our senses are. All natural resources depend on this force.
The usage of World divides into 3 types. Sense and time management of space and the reasons for the imbalance in it – the other subjects: The souls. If we imagine that reality is a time-space continuum and those who can feel it, and manage it are concentration points that are still part of it.
Using the World constitutes manipulation over everything that is in that time-continuum. This "everything" could be present as energy and matter. The way of controlling that everything is managing everything in each of its constituent elements: Earth, Fire, Air and Water. (This manipulation can be present as a manipulation over matter (or energy) of objects in solid, liquid, gas and plasma physical form).
Using our Will is a control over ourselves. Those who have free Will can reach certain States of mind. There are many States of mind, but their manifestation can be describe by only two States. Each of them opposite to the other by idea: Concentration (try to shut everything around us without our target point) and Harmony (attempt to merge yourself with the surrounding world).
As said, the sum of the amount of Stats is always a constant number for each species. In “Dimensions”, this constant is 40 for the playable races (our heroes).

1. BASICS

1.1. Stats and elements. Character card

The 4 Stats Vitality, World, Matter and the Will determine your uniqueness. This is your identity. They are constant. As mentioned, the sum of the four Stats is 40 points. 
You can change your card that represents your character, if your character passed away (and you do not have a tower or do not want to continue with this character), take a new character and join the game again.
Your character card has Stats distribution and special info like which element has specialized skill or another. <X> Adept means that your character begins the game as the master of its tower (and receives bonuses from it) and can go up to the tower in question whether its Stats are high or not.

1.2. Game initial setting: cards on the field - creatures and effects in the field.

Rule 0 (Initial setup tileset rule): The standard 3x4 tileset from the deck of the effects, depose two cards face down in each field. Depose on each tile one creature or one skill card at random. Randomly depose two class cards over the tiles occupied by monsters.  
Then you choose on which trap to depose your character clockwise, starting with the character with the highest Will (The character is counted as a Move action,and you may play Instants during this action). The player puts his character on a tile at least two tiles away from the enemy. If you put your character on the monster occupied  tile, you are in immediate combat with it: Apply the effects as traps and put them face up on that tile simulating the monster's hand.

Rule 0 (Initial setup character deck rule): For each player
Draw two character cards, choose one and discard the other (Your character card enters the tileset).
-Deal 10 effect cards and 4 skill cards:  You may put any of them in your deck and the rest in your archives.
- Deal 2 class cards: discard one and put the other one initially on board over your face up archive column.
- Draw as many Attack, Block / Move / Posture cards as you want to create a deck of minimum 15 cards.

Rule1 (action/round rule): Each character has three actions (turns) per round (attack, block, move or posture). However, the monster’s actions count written right down. Monsters with four actions per round forces you to replace one action with empty Posture*.
- The player may decide on which round to enter the Dimension tileset. She/He may cast permanents outside the dimension and on the proper round may find its right effects in hand in order to be ready to enter onto the desired tileset. 

Rule2 (discover tile rule): Once Attacked/(Moved towards) the location (tile) turns face up and it becomes "open" (if there is a creature there it is awaken, if there are only effects there, this tile is a trap set with 20 power for each element used in the effects). All the effects are activated once as traps.

Rule3 (monster rule): Awaken creature defends its tile and never leaves it. They attack any character who is in contact. In case there is an awakened creature, put all effects into those defender’s hands. (All defenders use D6 randomized actions. Read rule 5). Monsters regenerate if no enemy is in contact with them. The next enemy player controls the monsters (the player after the one that is in contact with them).

Rule4 (traps rule): To disable a trap at least once all the effects there must fail to kill you  (You survive the Damage done) or lock you (because of the high character’s Stats). Enough is one of the elements of the effect not to work and the effect does not work. (If a trap or a monster kills or "locks" your character you lose one time and can enter the Dimension once again fighting for Remi (draw). All traps are resolved in one round.

Rule5 (Monsters Dice randomized actions): Each monster in contact with your character throws typically 4 dice to determine its current action: green (block), blue/white (move),  red(attack) and black (posture) dice (D6). Some monsters have custom behaviour like for example BAAPM, AAAM, … , so they throw those combinations of D6 each action. When the Posture score is highest the monster can choose the proper effect from his hand and not throw dice until it emits all the effect elements.

Rule6 (empty posture/empty move rule): If the hero cannot or does not want to do action, you play an empty action (or no card) named Empty Posture. You do not receive the benefits of posture (does not beat Move). However, still you can make effects during the Empty posture. (Minor time interval. you can still do effects and skills or to wait in their absence). However you may play the Empty Posture card (that card always returns to your hand after an action evaluation) as an Empty Move action (declared first). For the sake of combat calculations the enemy chooses whether the Empty card type: Empty Move or Empty Posture (Enemy with Posture will choose to beat Empty Move, and enemy with Attack will choose to beat Empty Posture)

Rule7 (turn/round sequence rule): The characters make their actions simultaneously each turn (put their action cards face down). The turn sequence: 
1) all characters and creatures in contact put their cards face down, each turn.
2) All face down cards are turned face up and the actions are resolved (you can still play instants* and skills* before action resolving’s). You point out your target if any and then try to deal damage.
The characters and creatures in contact can play effects over their Posture (or empty posture*) actions.

- After the final turn from this round, the characters may discard any remaining cards from their hands, and then draw cards up to five. (Monsters do not discard, they always keep their cards in hand).
The characters or monsters in contact may choose or be forced to play empty Posture (It cannot be played like empty move when forced) in case the opponent has more actions per round, then they are able to perform.

Rule8 (card type declaring sequence rule): Each action(turn) from the round the player first declare:
- The Empty Move card(special way of playing Empty posture card) by just moving your character halfway towards the target tile. The Empty Move action is declared first when evaluation actions.
- Turn face up an action card: Attack, Block, Move, Posture or Empty Posture cards.
- Play Effects. Effect declaring is bounding your action as Emit a specific (first as ordered in effect description) element from the effect. There is no stack, so all skills and effects apply. In case that some effect or skill is prohibiting some action or emitting an element it is applied finally as replacement.
- Play skills, Instants, Class-card per-round-abilities or point buy an element/color in order to try to destroy a token.

Rule9 (class card rule): You enter the dimension with your class card as a permanent. It is always casted with tokens with a value of 6 for each color. Some classes are once per round, some have a static ability applied all the time, and some of them have a rechargeable ability that requires a tapping of the class card. To Untap such a class card you have to emit each color of the class (ignoring the number of elements of the same color). The Untaping can be performed even in different rounds (no interrupt is triggered). 

1.3. Your character is playing deck. The order of the actions.

Each character has a deck of playing which must contain at least 15 cards. The character can make exactly these actions. The actions are four types: attack, block, move and posture. Your deck can also contain a set of skills and effects. These actions order assembles so-called combos. The principle for assessing the actions of the opposition is the principle of (stone -> scissors -> paper -> stone). In our case attack-> posture-> move-> block-> attack. 

Each deck describes the fighting style of the player. In the stack can dominate arbitrary actions and each player chooses a character who will follow these steps as follows: 

Rule10 (draw rule): Each round hero draws cards until they complete their cards to five. Used cards go into the "discard pile of the deck" of the character. In addition, when there are no more cards in the deck, you reshuffle your discard pile and put it as your deck again. You may choose not to reshuffle your deck.

Rule11 (archive rule): A character may leave used cards in the archive instead of discarding that card (in case you want to refine your deck). The defeated monsters also go to the deck archive, and may be used by effects as necromancy, summoning (this creature instead of random library creature), possess, link .. as an outer dimension ally. When the ally monster enters this dimension it enters in your tile, moves with you and acts as a monster managed by you in case of decision making. You may always add the archived card in your discard pile.

Rule12 (permanent types rule): You put cards when played once into your discard pile. 
- A card with permanent type is put in front of your deck, until destroyed. You have to point buy* (roll dice with a sum less or equal to your Stats and put the highest dice as a defence token with that color. You may willingly repeat the rolling if the dice values do not satisfy you). Put over the permanent the small neutral dice with value from the color with the least value.
- The tile token is a big colored dice put on the right side of the square tile (your character card is in rectangle shape), It is also determined on a Point buy rule, and its value is added as a bonus for each creature over that tile.
- The Permanent token is a colored dice positionized over the specific stat of your character card and is applied as a stat bonus. 
 - The stat penalty token is a wooden dice and uses the same rule with the difference that it is put below the card stat number, and of course is subtracted from the stat when calculating the final stat.

Evaluation of the effects, skills and damages:

Rule13 (evaluation rule): Each turn (from the round), you oppose your actions, effects and skills to evaluate who benefits and who can cause damage. The effect evaluation is done after emitting or  point buy* all the elements of the effect. Combo evaluation is done when performing all the actions of it. For each turn/action the sum is rounded up and applied as damage.

Rule14 (acquisition/trade rule): Acquisition of effects and skills can happen with conquering a field, which has an effect or obtaining an effect from the deck of another character when you defeat him. You put the acquired effect (or skill) into your discard pile or archive pile. You may spend an action to trade your archive with another character archive if you are in adjacent tiles and one of you moves towards the another.
In case of an adjustment hero nearby you may also discard your cards into his discard pile, or put just played card in his discard pile instead of yours. 

Why do all beings take damage upon successful action? Where are the weapons, and why is there no separate fire and water protection of the character?
For simplicity, all playing characters and monsters do one damage on successful action. If a monster, because of its size or unique skills, would make more damage, this feature is mentioned in the monster’s card (example: when this creature makes a successful “Attack” => +1 bonus damage). On the other hand, if a creature does less damage than one, the system neglects that damage. (This creature is not worthy to be a „monster card").
For example, one untrained goblin would mean for the described characters the same as some fly to a man. Other examples: old trees have zero Will, high vitality, but for example Σ Stats = 20 => use them as "terrain map" with some effect, for example.
In Dimensions also for simplicity, we are not dealing with the type of weapon or hero’s armor. Details about why the creatures make one damage (with their bow, sword or horns) and why for example the characters have four life tokens would only complicate the game mechanics. Nevertheless, the characters could find skill cards and tokens that actually are unique items giving the owner described skill. In principle "you get what you see“, (If your character is drawn with an axe means that he can defend and attack i.e. with this axe. Availability of your axe makes your character completed. Questions such as how much damage you will do if you wear a longer axe or bow, are of no interest to the game mechanics.)
The creatures are described with their Stats, rather than mastery in each of the elements of simplicity. In principle, it is possible that some creature (a Demon or Fire elemental) is vulnerable to water but invulnerable to fire. However, systematically the demon would have high Ethereality, which helps him protect from water elements. Such assumptions are also made in order to simplify the system.
 
2. Battle System

2.1. Character’s life and damage.

Rule14 (life rule):The life of each creature (or character) is equal to the sum of its Stats divided by ten and rounded up. All the characters have Σ Stats equal to 40 so all characters have four life (they have four life tokens, for tracking their life. Monsters usually have Σ Stats. (For example, the Dragon’s life is eight life tokens). When damage by effect is done it is rounded up once effect is casted.
-- Total life token is a small neutral dice recalculated and updated total whenever a damage is done or whenever a new token of any kind is added/updated over your tile or over your character and its value is 5+ (so it can be rounded up). 

Rule14 (adjacent rule):  Characters can be attacked only by another character or creature around him (horizontal, vertical and diagonal).

2.2. Actions and combinations (combos).
 
There are four types of actions (that you can use to make your combo):

• Posture (Waiting / Skill / Effect / Aiming) Basis mental States (Specification of the posture), in which, e.g. wait enemy attack, ready to counter-attack. Skills made with posture impacts over the enemy mentally like: intimidation, inspire anger, provocation of the enemy, bluff etc. This action is the mental attitude with which the hero makes the effects. Only during the character's posture, you can make effects unless the effect does not say "instant" (Cast it anytime, even interrupting the enemy’s action. No need for special attitude and attention). ((P)). 

• Attack - "Dimensions" is not concerned with the type of the attack or the weapon. Attack is an aggression in all of its manifestations, following its goal*. Attack’s Skills specifies the type of the attack or the uniqueness of the weapon. ((A)). 

• Block - "Dimensions" does not deal with the calculation of the protection of the hero, because of equipment. Block represents any kind of defense that your character performs. Block‘s Skills usually are defensive skills that prevent any enemy trying to attack you and counter the attack. ((B)). 

• Move - body movement and / or movement on the tile-set. Specify movement Skills to describe the maneuver or interaction with the body of the enemy. ((M)). 

as described earlier in principle rock-scissors-paper: mental State is faster than the movement. Movement is "opportunity for maneuver" while the enemy defends. Defense is the best answer to the Aggression. Moreover, aggression is the best solution against mental conditions.

Rule15 (target/follow rule): With every action hero can target a field (move) and/or an enemy(attack, move). 
Only Block action does not target but instead performs one damage to each enemy who attacked you. (Using empty Posture, you also cannot perform targeting with effect or skill). If two characters target one tile and should be moved over it the only one that is using Move is moved there, or in case of mirror actions no one is moved there.
Using Attack action, you may follow (optional) the targeted hero if he moves, as if you perform a Move targeting the enemy’s tile. (I.e. you may chase a running enemy, alternating Attack and Move.  However, you may stay on your tile if you target the enemy/tile with Attack/Move (For example if you are channeling an effect* (point buy. Explained below).

Rule16 (combo rule): In addition, your character has a special combo (A sequence of trained actions in specific order). If you make your character combo and have contact with an enemy, you deal one bonus damage to him. Damage is applicable only while you are in contact with the opponents. Announce combos on their full execution. 
FYI: Adept means that character is using that specific element with power 20.

Why combos?
Combo represents a series of memorized movements you are trying to do in / at one (or more) enemy (without thinking). This combination is the idea with which you go into battle. However, it does not mean that your character actually does just that. The main purpose of your character is not to be killed, so he changes (without the player to be told) an action of his combo that can lead to fatal. This "changing" cost the hero / or give the enemy + "..." Bonus, converted to damage.
Monsters that character meets usually know one combo. Once they meet, a hero will know how to deal with them precisely next time.
Remember: to put the necessary modifications to the bonus and any tactical advantages - terrain type, cover, special features etc. 
The Combo is calculated thus: the first action, the second action, the third action, {fourth action}. You can find your combo on your character card. Watch out as a struggling skillful enemy, it can hit you two, three or four times per round.
The characters have a combo priori, but can find other combo cards in the process of game (in the expansion of the Dimensions will be included “combo cards” along with “events cards”, “terrain cards” and “item cards”).

Rule18 (element emit rule): Each effect comprises one or more elements. Character casts these effects by Emitting each element flow with power equal to his Stat. The enemy resists with his Stats. (Except the point buy effects, which cannot be resisted). If the Stat power of one of the elements of the effects used by you is less than or equal to the enemy’s Stat’s power, the effect is not successful (The enemy saves from the effect special remarks), or applies zero damage with that element in calculations.

Rule19 (effect cast rule): To cast the effect one or more elements are needed. The effects are casted with as many Postures as the number of elements needed for casting the effect. Once casted (or announced), if you spend contiguous* Postures to fulfil the effect’s requirements the effect succeeds, otherwise it fails. Then the effect goes to your deck’s discard pile, unless it is Channeled effect or effect with permanent Status. Channeling effect may continue as long as the character is on the same tile (you use the environment to channel the effect). Once you cast a permanent effect, put it in front of the character’s deck as permanent. The instant* effects are exceptions (read below). Postures must be contiguous to emit contiguous elements (you cannot cast an effect requiring two elements by using one posture, followed by one block, followed by another posture).

Rule20 (effect types rule): - Effects can have multiple types, where Point buy specifies that the Damage is done in Point buy way instead of Stat emit comparing way.
- Damage effects apply the summarized damage by Stat comparing for each element spent.
- Instant effects are castable with any other action, not only with "posture". 
 - Shield token: Add the point buy as dice value (rounded up) from the emitted  color as a transparent colorless dice over the tile (right from your character card). That token stands as a tile-based extra life and is cumulative.
- For just Damage/Instant/non-Damage effects put a dice with value “1” in the emitted element color over that castable card until the effect is casted or  in case of non damage effect the effect condition is no longer valid).
- Point buy: Roll dices with a sum less or equal to your Stats and choose the highest dice. It can be applied as a defence token/stat penalty (with that color) over the permanent/character/tile or applies the damage in case there is type Damage in addition. If your sum of the rolled dices is higher than your stat (emit fail) you can repeat the dice rolling (for that element. You may also choose to repeat the rolling if the dice values do not satisfy you) next action (Slower casting) as a contiguous action (Add the point buy as dice value in the emitted element color over that castable card).
- (channelling):  Continuously casting the same effect next turn/s (even in the next round). Turns the effect as a temporary permanent with point buy* defence tokens. That effect is put in your discard pile once you stop challenging or someone destroys its last defence tokens (add the point buy as dice value in the emitted element color over that castable card as last defence token. The channelling can be interrupted if that defense token is destroyed). 
- Stat buy: effects summarize the points spent to emit some elements to reach some Sum in a couple of rounds (add the stat as dice value in the emitted element color over that castable card).

Rule21 (effect damage rule): If the effect does Damage, The difference in Stats (yours and enemies) are equal to the damage.
The Effect’s Damage is divided by 10 and rounded up at mathematical principle, calculated each turn for the completed effects (in case the defender has no tokens for destroying. The defender chooses which token will be destroyed). If at the effect resolution it makes the difference 3 fire and water 2 difference, it still makes 2+3 points rounded up to 10 points difference = 1 damage. If the instantaneous effect makes 2 points and 2 points the next action, the effect at the effect resolution makes four points later rounded down to zero damage.

Rule22 (multicast rule): Just like doubled effects you can cast different effects simultaneously, as long as they require the same elements (you are actually emitting an element, announce as many effects as if you think you can cast). If two effects contain a common element then the character can cast them in three steps. Emit the first uncommon element and announce the first effect, then emit the shared element and announce the second effect (the first effect casting succeeds), then emit the second uncommon element (the second effect casting succeeds too). The character can superimpose multiple effects with shared elements simultaneously or chain them.

Rule23 (double cast rule): If a character has two effects with the same name, he may cast them both using the multicast rule.
In some doubled effects hero can achieve special rule described with the ∀ symbol. (“every” effect, in this case is at least two).

Rule24 (destroy permanent/token rule): An opponent can destroy any permanent effect by point buy* one of the elements used to make the effect, and the dice is higher or equal than the tone one. Any token  can be destroyed by point buy*.
You cannot target more than one permanent or token (unless you have an Aura permanent).
You choose either to emit an element or target one permanent/token, but not both (unless you have an Aura permanent).
Only the class card needs to destroy all the colors (ignoring the number of elements per color) against the value of 6.  

Rule25 (Tile-set initial deploy rule): Over a tile-set 3x4 (4x4 or 5x5, depending on the count of the players): - First distribute two effects on each tile as traps. 
- Then shuffle the monsters and skills cards in one pile and distribute at random one more card of them over each tile.
- Finally each player may choose its starting entry point in order determined by the Will stat. Every next player cannot choose a location that is nearby any other non-ally player. Remember that you cannot choose an entry point that has a monster (green) card deposed already, and you still have to resolve the entry point traps.

The count of the Effects is 50; each of them repeated 2 to 3 times. If the hero holds in his hand two effects of the same type, it can double one of the Stats required for the effect (For example if you have 15 Matter and World 15, and want to do balefire (fire, earth, time), it will do more have a material 30 and 15 World. If the goal is 10 matter and 10 World => [30 (matter) - 10 (fire) - 10 (land)] + [15 (World) - 10 (time)] = [10] + [5] => effect is successful and will do 15 damage.

8. Other game modes (PC version conception modes)

8.1. Arena
4x4 tiles box opposing two characters at the center diagonally. At the Nexus, there is an option for the Arena.
8.2. Dimension invasion (labyrinth style)/ Dimension protection (tower defense style)
Usually ~ 8x8 dimension with entry (nexus) and exit (library) point. The invaders enter at the entry point, and are heading to go to the exit point. 
The dimension creator cannot put two neighboring monsters and have limited walls to use. The severity of the invaders depends on the number cards used to make the dimension.

8.3. Raid (versus the system, or versus player dimension)
The characters are heading together to the final boss (Dragon) sitting over his library, or versus the player's dimension. The raid has a difficulty level equal to the number of the cards owned by the dimension nexus owner.

8.4. World mode (board and PC game style)
The players create the terrain tile-set the very same way as it is created in the Carcassonne game. Each player is given 8 monster cards randomly in advance. Each turn any player puts a tile in a proper way on the tile-set, and can choose to put a monster into the terrain tile he plays (if there were no monster at this territory type already). After depleting the terrain tiles at each tile is placed randomly another card face down. The owner of the territories can add them to their deck or leave them there as a trap. The territories with more than one player’s monsters are no man’s land and all their cards remain in place until some monster claims them. Border territories cards should be claimed as well.
Each player may use his cards in hand in order to perform effects casting on its territory (as the territory monster casts them. The territory monster owner cast the effect using its stats) or nearby its monsters. Also the monsters use the 4 common basic decks. There is no single hero in this mode, you play with the monsters you have (but the tokens and permanents are applied to each monster you currently possess). All monsters act as guardians until you instruct them otherwise with your cards in hand or from “special building ability”. You still have five cards in your hand for the whole round, and can play one basic action per turn, so you must choose carefully to which territory or monster you will help each turn.
Therefore, you can prepare your deck from the cards from the territories you own, and protect your territories as soon as someone reaches the border tile. When a monster loses all his life tokens it is turned face down and if you lose all the monsters on your territory, the invader turns it up as his monster guarding his new territory.
 The game finishes when someone has 12 monsters.

The tiles are four types of whole terrain cards (Vitality, World, Ethereality and Will) with various bonuses over them.
Six types of two terrain types border cards (Vitality/World, Vitality/Ethereality, Vitality/Will, World/Ethereality, World/Will and Ethereality/Will).
Twelve types of three terrain border cards, and six types of four terrain border cards.
The 3 to 6 player’s game should have 102 terrain tiles: 24 two-terrain types’ border cards + 24 three-terrain types’ border cards + 48 whole terrain cards + 6 four-terrain types’ border cards.

8.5. MMORPG
Each player starts the game over a Library with 20 cards. Builds his base and explores his dimension, then uses dimension doors to jump into some enormous dimension (or dimension chain). Starts synchronize with his team members and enemies if any.
- Each player has a deck size: 15 min / 20 max. (So you can get up to five cards from a successful raid).
- You start the game with 20 random cards deck: 8 action cards, 2 skills cards, 2 mines, 4 effects, 1 nexus card, 1 trade center card, 1 tower card, and 1 building card. You can add action cards freely once per day.

Additional cards:
Mine (immovable, it is indestructible once found or created) – generates five coins per required element / day. It usually has three elements to start producing coins. If the mine requires Space, Fire and Fire (15 coins/day) you may deploy two effects: one that requires fire and space, and other effect that require fire and water. Two of these cards are in your starting deck (20 coins/day; 15 coins/day). In addition, you start with the right random 4 effects that handles mines to be working. Once deployed on a mine they are replaceable. You may have a couple of them in your dimension. 
Force field, Wall, River, Lava (terrain card) – you may generate an unpassable (with the chosen power 20) wall tokens nearby. (On any of the nine neighboring tiles). This card requires a single element and is deployed just like a trap. 
Tower (terrain card) – unmovable defender that attacks only the attacking creatures (Unlike the defender creatures who will fight each other on engage). The Tower may just give you some bonus.
Element bonus/penalty (terrain card) – generate and element bonus/penalty token (point buy) on any of the nine nearby tiles. (Deployed as a trap). Any creature on these tiles will get the bonus/penalty.
Dimension Nexus – hire other players for a raid; accept other players offer for a raid once per day. This card is in your starting deck. You may have couple of them in your dimension. Remember that your character’s value is equal to the number of the non-action cards in your deck. The raid can be over a neutral dimension, or over some player’s dimension, once you have an ally “dimension door chain” towards it. The intruders will enter in your dimension from this tile.
Dimension trade center – Each day you may offer a card ransom for coins, or buy a card for coins. The barter is possible only via the dimension stashes. This card is in your starting deck. You may have a couple of them in your dimension. (This tile is your first diplomacy entry).
*Dimension stash (*this is not a card; it is just a token mark) – holds up to five cards like any other place, but this place may exist in another [ally] dimension. Once per day you may redeploy/draw some cards. The idea is to store some of your valuable/useless cards somewhere else, without overpricing your dimension. (This could be a tough diplomacy joke). 
*Dimension door (*this is not a card; it is just a token mark) – A connection tile with an [ally or unknown] dimension. Your dimension deployed card number is the dimension value of your dimension. The severity of the attackers depends from this number. The intruders will know your dimension value and the number of the dimension doors in this dimension in advance.
You may open or close a dimension door from an unknown dimension toward your dimension any time you want, channeling “dimension value” rounds. (When you are at a victorious raid, and you find that you can pillage more than 5 cards. Extremely dangerous for you and your allies, because anyone may enter through this dimension door). Several players may channel in order to close the dimension door. 
Opening a dimension door on a chosen tile toward your ally is done immediately with his agreement.
You may send your character, your recruitments from the nexus and from the recruitment buildings (you control) through the dimension door. You may even set free and send your guardians away this way, making them neutral once again. Just like the dimension stash, you may have unlimited dimension doors but remember that this makes you vulnerable. Remember that channeling to open and opened dimension doors is visible by all the players in that dimension. You have time to react to the enemies’ act of opening the dimension door.
You may start the game in an occupied dimension. You will understand thatre is another player in your home dimension when he opens a dimension door. In addition, you and a friend of yours may start the game in one dimension.
*Library (*this is not a card; it is a pile of unknown cards) – 100 coins/card. This is the place from where you buy new cards. Whenever your hero is over this tile, he may mind control the guardians he owns, and make them use his deck, even Move out of their initial tile. Each library may have a dominating element. For example, as you buy a card from the Fire library, you will have a 30% chance to get a card with a Fire element. If you buy a card from a World Library you will get a 50% chance to get a card with one of the World elements. A raid is successful once you step over a library (you get teleported home). Your starting location is over some library.
Attraction building card, Recruitment center – A building that is part of many puzzles (from 5 parts).  A completed puzzle will give you a daily production power of this creature. (That creature number is not cumulative in days; He just has a home at your dimension, and can be sent to raids and as an ally help). In case you have just some parts of the puzzle the production table is as follows (always round up down):
- 1/5 => 1/ 5 days. Producing a random creature, part of this puzzle.
- 2/5 => 1/3 days. (40% ~ 33%)
- 3/5 => ½ days. (60% ~ 50%)
- 4/5 => ½ days. (80% ~ 50%)
- 5/5 => 1/day.
Whenever you put a card over a creature building that creature will have it in his hand, but may lose it when it dies and reborn just like any character. One of these cards is in your starting deck.
        Artifacts card – generates bonus when active as a permanent. The special about this card is that it is not set up as a trap; it is like a skill card. You may find it exploring your dimension, just like any other trap card, walls or guardians. Usually these cards are some initial character boost used to pump up you creatures later.

8.6. MMORPG puzzle card concept

Based on “Attraction building card, Recruitment center” (8.5. rule: 10) for the MMORPG those puzzle cards in addition to the terrain cards are forming your dimension. The puzzle cards are three general types with many subtypes.
 Terrain
Common buildings (listed above).
Road/Tunnel/Passage – Spend empty posture as move action instead of “Move”.
Fortress/Castle/Rampart… - an unreachable area (Carcassonne alike), unless the fortification is devastated (hit it 20 times for example or use a special card against it).
Outposts/Towers* – gives you a range of two, three or N tiles for actions you apply. Chess alike Horizontal/Vertical/Diagonal types.
Creature – each puzzle part is a creative dynamic compilation: 
Creature parts – forming the creature dominant abilities
Runes/Behavior/Power words – adding the dominant special powers of that creature.
Creature form – border parts giving a shape of the creature, forming a container for the other parts. Once that container is closed, the creature is compiled and creature cards are ready to be produced.
Building – puzzles from 4 to 1024 tiles. Each tile is a word of a wise sentence or a poem. 
The buildings are statically fixed and have to be built fully before they start functioning. The sentences/poems are in a couple of languages like themes. Some “words” may be used in forming different buildings from the same theme (language), but some words could be in different languages (completely unusable for your target building poem for example). There could exist expensive letter puzzle parts.
The buildings can generate effect/skill cards, quests, information about enemies and puzzle parts, knowledge about the possible in your server poems and best creature types …

Each time the new server pops up a neutral puzzle cards are generated, guarded by traps, impassable terrains and creatures. The new players are located at places close to the puzzle cards unreachable for the older players. Each player could specialize as puzzle seeker, puzzle arranger, older player’s mercenary, puzzle spy/intelligence, puzzle reseller, puzzle party rider (puzzle parts are gathered by enemy’s dimension like any other card on victory) …

After a server merge your server territory will be coupled in one continent/planet and the process is repeated. Besides there are many more possible combinations, the puzzle knowledge base will be updated and your alliances will be able to create bigger puzzles with stronger output.
The new players will be located in their nationality theme puzzle parts to reach areas, but after the server merge some continents/planets with the same nationalities could be neighbors (which is a big advantage). As a compensation, the smaller nationalities are tolerated and buffed with more puzzle parts per server.

Typically, the bigger puzzles require hard to find puzzle parts and in most of the cases: a couple of players, as alliance or clan, are building them.
        Puzzle parts gathering is a player/alliance ultimate goal and at the same time a tool for reaching that goal. Since there are no “stronger” effects or skills, the power level is determined by the output of the puzzles. It gives the player/alliance collateral advantage in battles/wars. The puzzle output is also a diplomacy exchange coin, a temptation for the mercenary players to join you as a private army, and the reasons for warring wars. Most of the time the puzzle output makes you rich and gives you powerful defense bonus, gives you a territory expansion, and rarely gives you an attacking advantage. Therefore, it makes the player /alliance’s dimension/s safer. 
Since you are guarding a territory with/from real players some betrayals and revolutions are possible.

8.8. Time management

Upon successful mode execution, you draw a random card from the Library (in addition to gaining/losing a card to casualty).
The players have a fog of war, and are synchronized once they are in contact.
Time management (real time): 
Each turn lasts minimum 5+ seconds and maximum 15 seconds.
1) The player has up to 10 seconds to put an action. Once he put the action he can use the remaining time for step (2)
2) Then up to 5 seconds to put off any effects. The player may end this step immediately.
The player may cast instants any time (this turn or the next).
3) At the end of the round (after the last round’s turn) you have 5 seconds to discard the unnecessary remaining cards. Then the game adds up to 5 cards. You may force the end of this step as well.

Therefore, each round lasts from 15 seconds to 50 seconds. At the elapsed time, some art and story comments are played by the game’s environment. For example if you click on an unoccupied location twice, you will try to use Move towards this tile and skip the effect casting (2). While your hero is travelling, you may discard the unnecessary cards in your hand if it is the third round.

Once two players found each another, the turn number is equalized to the player with the smallest turn number. The remaining time is equalized to the player with more remaining time. 
The interesting phenomenon is a team with a shared vision at the end of turn 3 finds a player at the beginning of turn 1. All connected player’s times are equalized to the beginning of turn 1. (They may lack of cards this round, but that is it)
8.9. Real-time MMORPG (options instead of cards)

Each player starts with 30 random cards deck. You may put creatures, effects, skills and basic cards in it.
You have 4 basic actions slots, with 4 skills upon them if you have any.
In addition, you have 4 effect cards slots. 
Whenever you cast a skill or effect, the new skill or effect is taking its place. Each active card has a stack of cards enlisted for it. Whenever you cast those skill or effect, it goes on the bottom of the stack.
Adding the same effect to your deck reduces the cooldown or increases the effect power (channeling). You may dual cast, multicast and channeling if you prepare those types of effects on some of those 4 slots.

Each player chooses his creature, and he got it fully equipped with random items.
In this game, there are no levels or stronger cards; there is no pay to win. Each new card gives you an option to choose different combinations, but when the battle starts, you go with your creature full of items and different card combinations as any other player.

PS: you may bring even creatures or buildings of the effect slots, but once casted they stay of this dimension unless you conquer it and draw them back later. In addition, they do not go on the bottom of the slot’s stack. Each stack is casted fully for 6 minutes. Having the bigger stack makes the cooldowns smaller. Also putting in the slot an effects, that require more elements makes this way your stack bigger.
With basic actions, the Move action allows your hero to run a limited time of 15 seconds. The Attack is allowing him to run towards a hero like a charge by default. This way if the hero is “tired” (no Move is used) or stands in a stance (a Pose is used) the attacker will hit him. Having more actions that are basic reduces the cooldown of those basic actions. You choose whether you will use the Pose action when casting the effect or not by some default state. Pay attention that a creature even with one block in the deck have it active initially, but once he use it to stop the attacker he will need 6 minutes to recharge those block action. The cooldowns are reduced in geometric progression (So having 6 Moves will reduce the cooldown 6min->3min->1,5min->45sec->23sec->12sec which is smaller than 15 seconds, so your hero may run all the time, but he will still be hit by someone prepared with Pose when entering into his tile).
The dimension is still split in tiles, so one square tile can be traversed in 15 seconds (one move action), or for 30 seconds if you are walking (no Move action is used).
